\section{Introduction}
\label{intro}
The prevailing paradigm in human-computer interaction for Large Language Models (LLMs) is conversational dialogue \edit{(e.g., OpenAI-ChatGPT, 2023, Google-Gemini,2025, Meta-LLama,2024)}. Within this paradigm, extensive research has been conducted on long-context understanding, multi-turn iteration, and in-context learning \edit{need to search corresponding papers} to align model outputs with user needs and human values \edit{(Ouyang et al., 2022; Bai et al., 2022)}. While these automated alignment strategies provide valuable algorithms and metrics, they often lack human involvement, making it difficult to align with the diverse and specific preferences of individual users. \edit{need reference}

We argue that alignment is not a one-off training task, but a continuous runtime process. Pre-training and RLHF provide a "base alignment" for the average user, but for every specific user, it is crucial to provide an option for them to choose during deployment. Further alignment requires steerability towards user intent at inference time.


In both academic testing and real-world applications, users frequently encounter scenarios where they must correct specific parts of a generated response (e.g., "This part is wrong, please modify it")\edit{can u find anything}. However, current conversational models typically treat the entire previous dialogue—including the erroneous segments—as context.\edit{long in context learning reference} This leads to two critical issues during re-generation: dialogue \textbf{Error Propagation} and \textbf{Ambiguity} in user Feedback\edit{hope to find a clue}. Without clear and detailed instructions provided by the users in multi-dialogues, some models often fail to judge which parts of the generation need to be preserved, which parts need to be modified. Meanwhile, users cannot predict what models resample in this round. In this interaction paradigm, the model's alignment is implicit, making it difficult to provide users with explicit alignment behavior. Furthermore, re-generating the entire response to fix a local error results in significant computational inefficiency and token wastage.

To address these challenges, research like Inference-Time Intervention(), TruthX() has made great progress in model inference and alignment. Compared to fully automated methods, in the field of human-computer interaction, we argue that effective cooperation requires shifting the paradigm from \textbf{"black-box"} prompting to \textbf{"glass-box"} steering. Merely communicating with models through dialogues is insufficient when models lacks introspection into \textit{what} the user truly needs. Instead, users can take advantage of the auto-regressive mechanism of language models, inspect the model's internal decision-making process and intervene precisely at the point of divergence.

Based on this insight, we propose \textbf{Rethink}, an interactive framework for \textbf{Fine-Grained Inference-Time Intervention}. Rethink bridges the gap between mechanistic interpretability and human intent by visualizing internal signals—such as token-level entropy and logit distributions—to expose the model's uncertainty. Crucially, it empowers users to act as a "surgeon," pinpointing the exact token where reasoning diverges. This \textbf{"Interactive Activation Steering"} capability allows users to physically truncate erroneous paths in the KV cache, effectively blocking error propagation and achieving precise alignment with human logic.

In summary, our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose a user-informed, process-oriented interaction paradigm that resolves error propagation and feedback ambiguity during generation. We formalise the "Interactive Activation Steering" mechanism and propose certain diagnostic metrics to guide human intervention, demonstrating effectiveness in model aligning user intent, model reasoning accuracy and token efficiency in the human-AI interaction era.
    \item We introduce Rethink, a modular system that operationalises mechanistic interpretability tools (Logit Lens, Entropy) into an interactive steering interface.
    \item We conduct extensive experiments on several kinds of datasets
\end{itemize}


\section{Related Work}
\label{related}

\textbf{Human-LLM Interaction} \hspace{1mm}
Current Human-LLM interaction is predominantly defined by chat-based interfaces, treating the model as a "black box" where users influence outputs solely through prompting. While effective for general tasks, this paradigm lacks the granularity required for complex reasoning, often leading to the "Gulf of Execution" where users struggle to map their intent to model operations. Recent HCI research has explored "glass-box" interfaces to bridge this gap. For instance, \textit{Graphologue} (Jiang et al., 2023) visualizes the reasoning process as a graph, allowing users to manage context more effectively. Similarly, tools like \textit{Co-Audit} (Liao et al., 2023) and \textit{Wordcraft} (Coenen et al., 2021) introduce "direct manipulation" capabilities, enabling users to edit or infill text.

However, these approaches typically operate on the surface level (text) or require users to manually construct prompts. They rarely expose the model's internal decision-making process, such as uncertainty or alternative token probabilities. Our method advances this by visualizing the model's internal state (e.g., entropy, logit distributions) and enabling "Interactive Activation Steering." This allows users to intervene not just on the output text, but on the generative trajectory itself, effectively acting as a "surgeon" to prune erroneous reasoning paths.

\noindent \textbf{Activation Steering} \hspace{1mm}
Steering LLM behavior at inference time without parameter updates has emerged as a powerful alignment technique. Activation Steering methods, such as \textit{Inference-Time Intervention (ITI)} (Li et al., 2023) and \textit{Representation Engineering (RepE)} (Zou et al., 2023), identify and manipulate "steering vectors" in the model's activation space to enforce behaviors like truthfulness or harmlessness. Complementary to this, decoding strategies like \textit{Contrastive Decoding} (Li et al., 2023) and \textit{DoLa} (Chuang et al., 2023) exploit layer-wise discrepancies (e.g., "Logit Lens") to reduce hallucinations and improve factuality.

Despite their success, these methods are predominantly \textit{static} (applying a fixed vector globally) or \textit{fully automated}, lacking the adaptability to handle ambiguous or context-dependent errors. They often treat all tokens equally or rely on heuristic triggers. \textbf{Rethink} introduces a human-in-the-loop approach to steering. By computing a dynamic "Steering Opportunity Score," Rethink identifies specific moments of internal conflict and empowers users to apply precise, local interventions (via KV cache truncation or token selection), combining the scalability of automated metrics with the semantic judgment of humans.

\noindent \textbf{Process Supervision} \hspace{1mm}
Moving beyond outcome-based supervision, recent work views LLM reasoning as a search process. Methods like \textit{Tree of Thoughts (ToT)} (Yao et al., 2023) and \textit{Graph of Thoughts} (Besta et al., 2023) utilize branching and backtracking to explore diverse reasoning paths. These are often coupled with \textit{Process Reward Models (PRM)} (Lightman et al., 2023), which score intermediate steps to guide the search, effectively applying Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) principles to language generation.



\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Overview}
We propose \textbf{Rethink}, a framework designed to shift LLM interaction from "black-box" prompting to "glass-box" steering. Unlike standard conversational interfaces where users only see the final output, Rethink exposes the model's internal decision-making process—specifically its uncertainty and latent semantic shifts—allowing users to intervene during the generation process.
The framework consists of three coupled modules: the \textit{Generator}, which manages the autoregressive inference and KV cache; the \textit{Analyzer}, which computes real-time diagnostic metrics (e.g., entropy, logit divergence); and the \textit{Controller}, which provides the interface for users to execute "surgical" interventions. This design enables a tight feedback loop where human intent directly modulates the model's activation states.

\subsection{Generation and State Space}
We model the generation process as a trajectory through a high-dimensional state space. At time step $t$, the model's state $S_t$ is encapsulated by the Key-Value (KV) cache of all preceding tokens, denoted as $S_t = \{(K_i, V_i)\}_{i=1}^{t-1}$. The probability of the next token $y_t$ is conditioned on this state and the input $x$:
\begin{equation}
 P(y_t | y_{<t}, x) = \text{softmax}(W_U \cdot f(S_t, y_{t-1}))
\end{equation}
where $f$ represents the Transformer forward pass and $W_U$ is the unembedding matrix. Standard generation extends this trajectory linearly: $S_{t+1} = S_t \cup \{(K_t, V_t)\}$. Rethink interrupts this linear flow, treating $S_t$ as a mutable object that can be inspected and pruned.

\subsection{Diagnostic Metrics: The "Glass-Box" View}
To guide user intervention, we must translate high-dimensional internal states into interpretable signals. We employ two primary lenses:

\paragraph{Internal Conflict and Logit Lens.}
While final-layer probabilities reflect the model's committed output, intermediate layers often reveal suppressed alternatives. We utilize the \textit{Logit Lens} technique to project hidden states from layer $l$ into the vocabulary space, obtaining a distribution $P^{(l)}$. We quantify \textbf{Internal Conflict} as the divergence between an early layer's "intuition" and the final output:
\begin{equation}
    D_{\text{conflict}}(t) = D_{KL}(P^{(L)}_{t} || P^{(l)}_{t})
\end{equation}
A high divergence suggests the model "changed its mind" during processing, often indicating hallucination or sycophancy.

\paragraph{Steering Opportunity Score (SOS).}
Raw entropy is often noisy; a model may be uncertain between two synonyms (e.g., "happy" vs. "glad") which is benign. To identify meaningful intervention points, we propose the \textbf{Steering Opportunity Score (SOS)}, which weights internal conflict by semantic ambiguity:
\begin{equation}
    SOS(t) = \tanh(D_{\text{conflict}}(t)) \cdot (1 - \text{Sim}_{\text{sem}}(TopK))
\end{equation}
Here, $\text{Sim}_{\text{sem}}$ measures the cosine similarity of the embeddings of the top-$k$ tokens. A high SOS indicates a "bifurcation point" where the model is conflicted between semantically distinct paths, signaling a high-leverage opportunity for human steering.

\subsection{Interactive Intervention Mechanisms}
Rethink provides a set of operators that manipulate the generative trajectory. Formally, we define an intervention operator $\mathcal{I}(S_t, a)$ that maps a current state $S_t$ and user action $a$ to a new state $S'$.

\paragraph{KV Cache Truncation (Rewind).}
When a user identifies an error at step $t$, simply editing the text is insufficient as the error's hidden state remains in the context. Rethink performs a physical \textbf{Rewind}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{I}(S_{t+k}, \text{truncate}(t)) = S_t
\end{equation}
This operation discards all KV pairs for steps $>t$, effectively resetting the model's memory to the moment before the error occurred. This guarantees that the re-generation is conditioned purely on the correct prefix, eliminating error propagation.

\paragraph{Steering and Resampling.}
Beyond truncation, users can force the model to explore alternative paths. The \textbf{Steering} operator allows the user to select a token $y'_t$ from the top-$k$ candidates (revealed by the Logit Lens) that aligns with their intent. The trajectory then branches:
\begin{equation}
    S'_{t+1} = S_t \cup \{(K(y'_t), V(y'_t))\}
\end{equation}
This transforms the generation from a single chain into a user-guided tree search, where the human acts as the value function.

\section{Experiments}

To validate the proposed "Glass-box Steering" paradigm, we design experiments across three dimensions: \textbf{Theoretical Feasibility} (via Simulation), \textbf{Cooperative Robustness} (via Sycophancy Tests), and \textbf{User Experience} (via Human Study). We aim to demonstrate that exposing internal states not only improves reasoning accuracy but also empowers users with the "Right to Know" and "Right to Choose."

\paragraph{Experimental Setup.}
We instantiated the Rethink framework using \textbf{Llama-3-8B-Instruct} as the backbone model. For diagnostic metrics, we computed \textbf{Internal Conflict} by measuring the KL divergence between the logits of the middle layer (Layer 16) and the final layer. The \textbf{Steering Opportunity Score (SOS)} threshold for automated interventions was set to $\tau=0.3$.

\subsection{RQ1: Theoretical Feasibility (Simulation)}
\textbf{Can "White-box Steering" theoretically outperform "Black-box Prompting"?}
To establish an upper bound for our method without the variability of human fatigue, we developed a \textbf{Simulation Engine} acting as a "Virtual User" that triggers interventions based on Ground Truth or SOS signals.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Baselines:}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Standard Generation:} Zero-shot CoT.
        \item \textbf{Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023):} Automated self-correction via prompting ("You are wrong, fix it").
        \item \textbf{Oracle Prompting:} A strong baseline where the user identifies the exact error location $t^*$ but is restricted to natural language feedback. This isolates the benefit of the \textit{Intervention Mechanism} (KV manipulation).
    \end{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Metrics:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Correction Success Rate (CSR):} Percentage of errors successfully fixed.
        \item \textbf{Token Saving Rate (TSR):} $1 - \frac{Tokens_{Rethink}}{Tokens_{Prompt}}$. We hypothesize that Rethink significantly reduces computational cost by avoiding the re-generation of correct prefixes.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RQ2: Cooperative Robustness (Resisting Sycophancy)}
\textbf{Can internal signals help users resist model bias and "Yes-Man" behavior?}
In Human-AI cooperation, a critical failure mode is \textit{Sycophancy}, where the model blindly agrees with a user's erroneous belief. We argue that a "Glass-box" system should use its internal confidence to alert the user.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scenario:} We take correctly answered questions from GSM8K and simulate a user challenging the model: "I think the answer is [Wrong Answer]. Are you sure?"
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Black-box (Baseline):} Standard models often apologize and flip to the wrong answer.
        \item \textbf{Glass-box (Rethink):} The system checks the \textbf{Internal Conflict} (Entropy/Logit Gap) of the original answer. If the model was internally confident (Low Conflict), it refuses to flip, effectively saying, "My internal state suggests I am correct."
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Metric:} \textbf{Flip Rate} (Lower is better). This measures the system's ability to act as a reliable partner rather than a sycophant.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RQ3: User Agency \& Experience (Human Study)}
\textbf{Does the "Right to Know" (Visualization) and "Right to Choose" (Intervention) actually empower users?}
We conducted a pilot user study (N=10) to evaluate the qualitative impact of the Rethink interface.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Task:} Participants were asked to fix 5 complex reasoning errors in the Math500 dataset.
    \item \textbf{Conditions (Within-Subjects):}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Chat Mode (Baseline):} Standard conversational interface.
        \item \textbf{Rethink Mode (Ours):} Interface with SOS Heatmap (Right to Know) and Click-to-Truncate (Right to Choose).
    \end{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Quantitative Metrics:} Time-to-fix and Number of Interaction Turns.
    \item \textbf{Qualitative Analysis:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Perceived Control:} Likert scale rating (1-5) on "I felt in control of the generation."
        \item \textbf{Transparency:} Likert scale rating (1-5) on "I understood why the model made the mistake."
        \item \textbf{"Aha!" Moments:} We analyze user logs to identify cases where the SOS Heatmap successfully drew the user's attention to a subtle error (e.g., a hallucinated entity) that was missed in the text-only view.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
The construction of the Rethink framework is grounded in the philosophy of \textbf{"Software Engineering for AI"} and \textbf{"White-box Steering."} Just as developers use breakpoints and step-by-step debugging in an IDE to understand code execution, we ask: can we apply similar principles to LLM generation? Rethink answers this by providing a framework that lowers the cost of debugging model outputs. This conversational, human-in-the-loop paradigm empowers users with deep insight into the model's internal states, offering higher controllability and interpretability than traditional black-box methods.

\section{Limitations}
Due to time and resource constraints, this work is presented as a reproducible demo rather than a comprehensive system like LLaMA Factory. We have not yet adapted the framework to a wide range of new models or built a runtime environment suitable for diverse deployment scenarios. Additionally, storing layer-wise logits and hidden states requires significant memory and computational resources. Finally, as a human-in-the-loop approach, Rethink incurs a higher labor cost compared to fully automated methods, though it offers greater precision.

\section{Future Work}
The interaction paradigm introduced by Rethink can serve as an advanced "Debug Mode" for users with high requirements for reasoning accuracy and preference alignment. Future work will focus on improving the \textbf{Steering Opportunity Score (SOS)} and recommendation algorithms to provide users with more fine-grained, personalized analysis and suggestions. We also aim to explore \textbf{automated steering} strategies that can learn from human interventions to reduce the need for manual oversight.
